# Review · English Domain stopline sample

Rendered with the installed `charles-store-screenshots/scripts/shots.py` at scale 3. Four RGB PNGs: 1320 × 2868. Final render reported no missing assets, failed fonts, console problems or seam CHECK warnings.

## Ten-item bar

1. **Pass — two-frame hook.** “Less tracking. More control.” is a two-beat promise. One paper ground spans frames 1–2 and the same domain trace crosses their seam. Frame 1 independently identifies AdVoid as an on-device ad/tracker blocker.
2. **Pass — crossed seams.** One SVG trace crosses all three seams and intentionally bleeds 80 CSS px beyond both strip ends. No word, phone or domain label is cut by a seam. Phones and crops have at least 28 CSS px horizontal clearance.
3. **Pass — one idea.** Frame 3: “See what gets blocked.” Frame 4: “Choose your blocklists.” Both are below six words. Frames 1–2 carry the hook plus one true product fact each.
4. **Pass — search readability.** Hook type is 80 CSS px, later headlines 54 px, facts and paid/demo disclosures 16 px. Contrast: ink/paper 13.79:1, emphasized green/paper 5.60:1, mint/ink 10.58:1, footer/paper 6.25:1. Headings were inspected in the generated search view on white and black surrounds.
5. **Pass for sample — real UI.** All screens and lifted crops come directly from the repository's genuine 1206×2622 captures. Screens are 340 CSS px wide. The blocked domain, 122 tile and blocklist rows are magnified source pixels, never reconstructed UI. Captures were not freshly recaptured on an iPhone during this task; refresh them against the release build before production use.
6. **Pass — named move.** “The domain underline” is present in every frame: the green request-like trace connects the blocking, activity and list-control moments. The actual AdVoid screens, blocked-domain proof and list selections make this more product-specific than a generic privacy shield poster. It could still be made more proprietary in a later brand exploration; the sample does not claim exclusive ownership of a line motif.
7. **Pass — consistent system.** One locally loaded typeface (Inter Tight), one upright phone geometry, one top light, one green word emphasis per headline. The hook has one shared ground; after the hook, the deliberate rhythm change is light/dark ground. Real capture content and the shape of its lifted excerpt vary by feature.
8. **Pass — people/assets.** No people, stock photos, generated imagery or external faces are used. Assets are real app captures and authored HTML/CSS/SVG geometry.
9. **Pass — tells checked.** See below.
10. **Pass — truthful proof.** No invented rating, review volume, award, rank or press quote. Frames 1–2 use product facts supported by the source: local on-device blocking and on-device activity history. Numeric captures are marked example activity, not presented as real-world effectiveness or user counts.

## Tells

- No gradient-ground/centered-phone template; the ground is flat and the set has a continuous seam-crossing trace.
- No purple/indigo ground, fake glossy 3D icons, invented badges, generated people or decorative neon.
- Headlines express outcomes, not “smart/advanced” feature adjectives.
- No text on busy photography; no photos are used.
- Captures show active protection, populated activity and configured lists, not login/onboarding/empty states.
- One phone scale and angle throughout. After the hook, ground changes keep the rhythm from becoming four identical cards.
- The green glow belongs to the actual Home screen's protection button, not added decorative light.
- Nothing covers the Protected status or the active blocked-domain proof. The lifted examples intentionally repeat the source fact at a readable size.
- No seams cut text or the phone screens. No near-edge slivers were reported; the activity crop was trimmed after full-size inspection to avoid a fragment of the next row.
- One emphasized word per headline. Footer strips were added after the first render to keep paid/demo disclosures off the UI and preserve contrast.

## Craft questions

- **Three-second read:** AdVoid blocks ads/trackers on the iPhone and lets the owner see and choose what gets blocked.
- **Seams:** shared ground + one trace at 1|2; same trace at 2|3 and 3|4.
- **One idea:** less tracking; control; visible blocked domains; trusted/customizable lists.
- **Swipe:** the two-beat hook resolves the promise, then frame 3 makes that promise concrete with the actual blocked request.
- **Logo swap:** a competitor logo alone would conflict with the visible AdVoid UI. The trace is category-relevant rather than a standalone proprietary brand mark; the app's actual evidence carries the distinction.
- **Closest reference:** Open's restraint, from the skill's reference sheet. This sample is stronger on direct product proof and weaker on established brand/social authority, which is deliberately not fabricated.
- **Levers:** processing fluency and aesthetic-usability in all frames; curiosity in 1–2; specificity in actual domain/list crops; isolation through one green word; peak-end through user control in frame 4.
